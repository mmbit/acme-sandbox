# Brand guides

Learned while making a company's brand from scratch: three looks shown as one film, a second round of two, and a
guide for the one picked. A company's own brand guide is its own; this note is how to make one.

## Making one

- **Explore a few looks, each by its own designer,** and add one editor. The editor first builds the shared tools
  (a stage that puts any logo on the real building or page, one caption style for the film), then cuts the film.
- **Same words, same data.** Give every look the same copy (one voice file) and the same sample catalogue (one data
  file). Then the looks compare on look, not on wording, and two designers reading one file can't drift apart.
- **Show it as a film walkthrough,** not a link: each look on a site, a newsletter, a sign and a product, then side by
  side. Captions, no voice. A second round narrows to two.
- **Design for scale.** Show each page with 5, 30 and 150 products. Anything drawn per product stops scaling: draw the
  company, and let data draw the products.
- **Test the mark at 16 pixels first,** and run the how-it-lands check on every mark (`cultural-check.md`,
  `pixel-art.md`).
- **The wordmark is drawn, not typed.** Never retype the name in a font to stand in for it.
- **Never two versions of the name on one screen.** A placeholder wordmark next to another version is the first thing
  a viewer notices.

## What goes in the guide

1. **The idea**, in two sentences.
2. **The logo:** colours on light and on dark, clear space, minimum size on screen and in print, how it scales (an SVG
   to any size, pixel images at whole numbers only), the small mark for icons, and the don'ts (no stretching, outlines,
   glows, busy backgrounds, mascot versions).
3. **Type:** the faces, their licence (self-hosted), sizes for desktop and phone, and the fallback for email.
4. **Colour:** a table of name, hex, use, and the contrast of every text pairing, checked.
5. **Pictures:** the style, with do's and don'ts.
6. **Where it appears:** the website, newsletters, signs, packaging, and how products carry the brand.
7. **The voice:** a few numbered rules and a do/don't table with real sentences (`copy.md`).
8. **Its honest weakness,** and the discipline that keeps it in check (a playful illustration style can read as a
   toy company unless the sentences stay calm and the art has room around it).
9. **Files:** where the logo files live and how they're built from one source.

## What holds in any brand

- **Whimsy lives in the craft and the details,** never in a voice that deflects. A mascot that jokes over a real
  problem reads as cynical.
- **When something goes wrong, the brand says so plainly** and respects people's intelligence.
- **Copy stays true at every size:** a heading that names a count is wrong the day one more arrives.
- **Mock copy written for a film isn't checked for publishing.** Check every factual claim again before it goes out.
